## What is protein?

Proteins are made up of amino acids linked together in different sequences.

The body uses amino acids to:

- Build and repair tissues
- Produce enzymes
- Produce many hormones and signalling molecules
- Support immune function
- Maintain muscle tissue
- Support skin and other structural tissues
- Contribute to numerous metabolic processes

Some amino acids can be synthesized by the body, while **essential amino acids** must be obtained from the diet.

This is why both the **amount and quality of dietary protein** matter.

## Why is protein important for muscle?

Muscle tissue is continuously undergoing processes of protein breakdown and protein synthesis.

Exercise, particularly resistance training, creates a stimulus for muscular adaptation. Adequate dietary protein provides amino acids required for muscle protein synthesis and tissue repair.

A simplified model is:

**Resistance exercise + adequate protein + recovery → muscular adaptation**

Protein alone, however, does not automatically produce muscle gain.

Muscle development depends on the interaction between:

- Resistance training
- Protein intake
- Total energy intake
- Sleep
- Recovery
- Training progression
- Individual physiology

## Protein and healthy ageing

Maintaining muscle becomes increasingly important with advancing age.

Age-related loss of muscle mass and strength can contribute to reduced physical function and independence.

Adequate protein intake, combined with appropriate physical activity—particularly resistance training—can support the maintenance of muscle and physical function in older adults.

The goal is therefore not simply to maximize muscle size but to preserve **strength, mobility and functional capacity**.

## How much protein do you need?

Protein requirements vary between individuals.

Factors that can influence requirements include:

- Age
- Body weight
- Physical activity
- Resistance training
- Pregnancy and lactation
- Overall energy intake
- Health status
- Recovery from illness or injury
- Specific nutritional goals

For healthy adults, a commonly used baseline reference is approximately **0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight per day**.

However, people who are physically active, older adults, or those pursuing specific training goals may require different amounts.

Therefore, a single protein target should not automatically be applied to everyone.

## Protein for people who exercise

People who regularly exercise may have higher protein requirements than sedentary individuals because training increases the need for tissue repair and adaptation.

Resistance training is particularly relevant because it creates a stimulus for muscle protein synthesis.

A practical approach is to distribute protein across meals rather than consuming almost all of it in one meal.

For an active individual, protein can be included in:

- Breakfast
- Lunch
- Evening meal
- Appropriate snacks

The overall daily intake remains more important than obsessing over a single meal.

## Protein and weight management

Protein can be useful during weight-management programmes because it contributes to satiety and helps support lean tissue.

When calorie intake is reduced for weight loss, preserving muscle becomes an important consideration.

A comprehensive weight-management strategy can combine:

**Appropriate energy intake + adequate protein + resistance training + aerobic activity + adequate recovery**

Protein should not be viewed as a "fat-burning nutrient."

Its value lies primarily in its role in nutrition, tissue maintenance, satiety and physical function.

## Animal and plant sources of protein

Protein can be obtained from both animal and plant foods.

### Animal sources

Examples include:

- Eggs
- Milk and dairy products
- Fish
- Poultry
- Meat

Many animal proteins provide all essential amino acids in relatively high proportions.

### Plant sources

Examples include:

- Lentils
- Beans
- Chickpeas
- Peas
- Soy
- Nuts
- Seeds
- Whole grains

A varied plant-based diet can also provide adequate protein when total intake and food variety are appropriate.

People following vegetarian or vegan diets should pay attention to overall protein intake and dietary variety.

## What is protein quality?

Protein quality refers broadly to the amino-acid composition, digestibility and biological usefulness of a protein source.

Essential amino acids are particularly important because the body cannot synthesize them in sufficient amounts.

Different foods have different amino-acid profiles.

For plant-based diets, combining a variety of protein-containing foods across the overall diet can help provide a broad range of amino acids.

The goal does not necessarily require combining specific foods at every individual meal.

## Should you take protein supplements?

Protein supplements are **not automatically necessary**.

Many people can meet their protein requirements through ordinary foods.

Supplements can be convenient when:

- Dietary protein intake is difficult to achieve
- A person has limited time for meal preparation
- Higher protein requirements make food-based intake inconvenient
- A convenient post-exercise option is desired

However, supplements should supplement the diet—not replace a varied diet unnecessarily.

The quality and safety of the product also matter.

## What about whey protein?

Whey is a milk-derived protein containing all essential amino acids.

It can be a convenient source of high-quality protein, particularly for people who exercise.

However:

**Whey protein is food supplementation, not a requirement for muscle development.**

Someone consuming adequate protein from eggs, dairy, pulses, legumes, meat, fish or other foods does not automatically need whey.

People with milk-protein allergy or significant lactose intolerance may need alternative options depending on the specific product and individual tolerance.

## Protein and hydration

Adequate hydration is important for overall health and normal physiological function.

People who increase their protein intake should continue maintaining appropriate fluid intake.

The idea that high protein intake automatically damages the kidneys should not be generalized without context.

People with **known kidney disease or other medical conditions affecting protein requirements** should obtain individualized dietary guidance rather than independently adopting a high-protein diet.

## Protein and kidney health

Protein requirements can be different in people with kidney disease.

A healthy person and a person with chronic kidney disease should not necessarily follow the same protein prescription.

Anyone with known kidney disease, significant liver disease or another condition affecting nutrition should discuss protein intake with an appropriate healthcare professional.

## How Ayurveda approaches protein-rich foods

Ayurveda does not classify food solely according to modern macronutrient categories.

Traditional Ayurvedic dietary assessment considers factors such as:

- **Ahara** and dietary quality
- Digestive capacity
- Appetite
- Food quantity
- Timing of meals
- Individual constitution
- Season
- Lifestyle
- Compatibility and tolerance

Modern nutrition describes foods in terms of protein, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, minerals and other components.

These frameworks should not be treated as identical, but they can be considered together when developing an individualized dietary approach.

From an Ayurvedic perspective, simply increasing a single nutrient without considering **Agni, Ahara, quantity, digestion and individual requirements** may not represent a complete approach to nutrition.

## How much protein should you eat in one meal?

There is no single universal amount that every person needs at every meal.

The appropriate amount depends on:

- Total daily protein requirement
- Body size
- Age
- Physical activity
- Training goals
- Number of meals
- Dietary pattern

For most people, distributing protein across meals can be a practical strategy rather than concentrating almost the entire daily intake into one meal.

## Common mistakes with protein

### Thinking more protein is always better

Protein is essential, but extremely high intake does not automatically produce proportionally greater benefits.

### Ignoring total diet quality

A diet can be high in protein while still being poor in fibre, micronutrients or overall dietary variety.

### Depending entirely on supplements

Protein powders are convenient, but they are not a replacement for a varied diet.

### Neglecting resistance training

If the goal is to maintain or increase muscle, protein works best alongside an appropriate training stimulus.

### Copying an athlete's protein intake

An athlete's nutritional requirements may differ substantially from those of a sedentary individual.

### Ignoring medical conditions

People with kidney disease and certain other conditions may require individualized protein recommendations.

## A practical way to improve protein intake

Instead of immediately calculating complicated nutrition targets, start by identifying the protein-containing foods already present in your diet.

For example:

**Breakfast →** eggs / dairy / legumes / other protein source

**Lunch →** dal / beans / dairy / other protein source

**Snack →** curd / milk / nuts / other suitable option

**Dinner →** pulses / dairy / eggs / fish / meat / soy or other protein source

Then assess whether the overall intake is appropriate for your body size, activity level and health status.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is protein necessary every day?

Yes. Protein is continuously required for normal physiological processes and tissue maintenance, so regular dietary protein intake is important.

### Can vegetarians get enough protein?

Yes. Vegetarian diets can provide adequate protein through foods such as pulses, beans, lentils, dairy products, soy, nuts, seeds and whole grains when the overall diet is appropriately planned.

### Do I need protein powder if I go to the gym?

Not necessarily. Protein powder is a convenient option, but many people can meet their protein requirements through ordinary foods.

### Is more protein better for muscle gain?

Not indefinitely. Muscle development depends on protein intake together with resistance training, adequate energy, recovery and other factors.

### Can high protein damage the kidneys?

Protein recommendations differ for people with kidney disease. Healthy individuals and people with chronic kidney disease should not automatically be given the same protein prescription. People with known kidney disease should seek individualized medical or dietary guidance.

### Should older adults pay more attention to protein?

Maintaining adequate protein intake becomes particularly relevant with ageing because preservation of muscle mass and strength is important for physical function and independence.

## Conclusion

Protein is a fundamental nutrient involved in tissue maintenance, muscle function, enzymes, hormones, immune processes and numerous other physiological functions.

However, **protein requirements are individual**.

A practical approach considers:

**Body size → age → physical activity → training → health status → dietary pattern → overall nutrition**

For people who exercise, adequate protein combined with appropriate training and recovery can support adaptation and preservation of lean tissue.

From an Ayurvedic perspective, protein should not be considered in isolation from **Ahara, Agni, quantity, digestion, routine and individual requirements**.

The goal is not simply to consume as much protein as possible, but to develop a **balanced, nutritionally adequate and sustainable dietary pattern appropriate to the individual**.
